# Artificial intelligence

*A book in 21 chapters about building agents that act well: searching, playing games, reasoning in logic, planning,
handling uncertainty, making decisions, learning from rewards, understanding language and moving in the world. Every
method is built from scratch in Python, proved where it can be, traced by hand, and followed by questions whose
answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Code.** Every program is short Python 3, sometimes with NumPy, and nothing else. Each chapter says in one line
  which tools do the same job in real projects.
- **The other books.** The [Algorithms book](../algorithms/index.md) covers graph search, shortest paths and
  NP-completeness in depth; the [Machine learning book](../machine-learning/index.md) covers the learning methods
  (neural networks, transformers) that this book's agents use. The chapters link to both instead of repeating them.
- **Do the questions.** Try each one before opening its answer.

## Three ways in

- **New to the subject:** read chapters 1 to 7. Agents and search are the core of classical AI and need only
  school math.
- **You want reasoning under uncertainty:** read chapters 2 and 3, then chapters 12 to 15.
- **You want today's systems:** read the Machine learning book's chapters 15 to 19 first, then chapters 15 to 17
  and 20 here.
