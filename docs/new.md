# What's new

*Recent results in algorithms, machine learning and AI, newest first, two lines each with a link. Last checked: 4 November 2026.*

## Algorithms

| When | What changed | More |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | **Shortest paths beat the sorting barrier.** Duan, Mao, Mao, Shu and Yin give an $O(m \log^{2/3} n)$ algorithm for directed graphs with non-negative lengths, the first to beat Dijkstra on sparse graphs (best paper, STOC 2025). | [Shortest routes guide](topics/Shortest-routes.md#now-and-next) |
| 2025 | **Time can be simulated in far less space.** Ryan Williams shows any computation of $t$ steps fits in about $\sqrt{t \log t}$ memory. | [Algorithms book, ch. 25](books/algorithms/25-Where-Algorithms-Go.md) |
| 2025 | **Faster open-addressing hash tables.** Farach-Colton, Krapivin and Kuszmaul disprove a 1985 conjecture of Yao about how fast such tables can be. | [Hash table](data-structures/hash-table.md) |
| 2024 | **Dijkstra is universally optimal.** Haeupler, Hladík, Rozhoň, Tarjan and Tětek prove it is as fast as possible on every graph, with the right heap (best paper, FOCS 2024). | [Dijkstra](graphs/dijkstra.md) |
| 2024 | **Rust 1.81** switches its sorts to driftsort and ipnsort. | [Sorting guide](topics/Sorting-and-searching.md) |
| 2023 | **AlphaDev** finds shorter routines for sorting 3 to 5 items; they are now in LLVM's C++ library. | [Sorting guide](topics/Sorting-and-searching.md#now-and-next) |
